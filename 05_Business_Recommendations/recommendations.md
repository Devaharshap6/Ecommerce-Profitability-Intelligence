# Business Recommendations

## 1. Prioritize freight optimization in Electronics

Electronics has a high freight-to-revenue ratio and low modeled contribution margin. Investigate carrier rates, routes, packaging, fulfillment choices and seller-level shipping economics before changing assortment.

## 2. Manage freight as a profitability KPI

Track `Freight / Revenue` by category, seller and route. Use economic scale alongside percentages so small-volume anomalies do not drive decisions.

## 3. Evaluate sellers on contribution, not revenue alone

High-revenue sellers with disproportionate freight can dilute contribution. Combine revenue, freight, order volume and modeled contribution in seller scorecards.

## 4. Connect delivery operations to customer experience

Monitor late-delivery rate alongside review scores. Prioritize high-volume sellers and lanes where operational friction coincides with weaker customer feedback.

## 5. Replace the modeled cost assumption with actual finance data

The 65% product-cost assumption is the principal limitation. A production model should ingest acquisition cost plus packaging, payment processing and other variable fulfillment costs.

## Scenario: Electronics freight reduction

A modeled 10% reduction in Electronics freight is estimated to save approximately **₹4,568** and increase current modeled contribution by approximately **53%**, assuming the 65% product-cost assumption remains valid. This is a scenario, not a guaranteed financial outcome.
